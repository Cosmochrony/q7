  \begin{abstract}
    Two independent sub-programmes of the Cosmochrony framework independently produce the integer
    three as a structural output.
    Paper Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} derives a four-dimensional effective Lorentzian geometry from the
    homogeneous dimension $\Dhom = 4$ of $\Heis(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ (Bass--Guivarch), with
    three spatial directions: the two horizontal generators $\tilde X, \tilde Y$ and the central
    element $\tilde Z = [\tilde X, \tilde Y]$ (the last entering via sub-principal corrections,
    open problem Q5b-O2).
    Paper O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} derives $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ from quaternionic minimality;
    papers O28--O29~\cite{Beau2026a32,Beau2026a33} establish that the admissible projection space
    $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ has rank $r_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3$ and houses a spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
    trajectory subspace $\Vr$ with $d_\rho = 2$.
    We identify a representation-theoretic mechanism that constrains any possible bridge between
    these two ``3''s.
    First, we prove that the bridge cannot be a Lie algebra isomorphism: $\su(2) \simeq \ImH$ is
    semisimple, while $\heis$ is nilpotent.
    Second, we show that $\Heff \simeq \Sym^2(\Vr)$ as an $\su(2)$-module (the spin-$1$
    representation), and that the irreducibility of $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ forces any equivariant map to the
    spatial sector to be unique up to positive scalar (Rigidity Lemma via Schur).
    Third, we prove a Schr\"{o}dinger Quadratic Form Lemma: the $\su(2)$ Casimir restricted to
    $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ pulls back, under the bridge map, to a positive-definite rank-$3$ quadratic form
    on the spatial block of the effective symbol, with the spin-weight grading $(\pm 1, 0)$ matching
    the Carnot grading $(1, 2)$ of $\heis$ in a precise sense.
    These results stop short of identifying the two ``3''s but they uniquely constrain the form any
    identification must take, and reduce the open question to a single computable criterion on
    $\sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})$ that simultaneously resolves Q5b-O2 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}.
    A new analytical result completes the picture: we prove that the chirped discrete Fourier
    transform $F_c$ (the metaplectic representative of the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ rotation) commutes
    with $L_{\mathrm{Weil}}$ for all $(q,c)$, which implies that the cross-term part of the
    criterion holds structurally.
    Numerical verification on O25 checkpoints for $q \in \{61, 101, 151\}$ confirms the vanishing
    of cross terms for all tested pairs, and the residual isotropy gap $|A_H - A_z|$ is observed
    to decrease monotonically with $q$ in the low-energy sector.
    All available analytical and numerical evidence is consistent with asymptotic isotropy
    $A_H = A_z$, reducing the identification problem to a single scalar convergence.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction and Statement of the Problem}

    \subsection{Context}

      The Cosmochrony programme derives physical observables from the spectral properties of
      admissible non-injective projections on the Weil representation of $\Heis(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
      Two sub-programmes have now independently produced the integer~$3$ as a structural output.

      \paragraph{The geometric ``3'' (Q-series).}
        Paper Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} establishes that the BFS shell stratification of
        $G_q = \Heis(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ converges, in the pre-saturation regime, to the
        Carnot--Carath\'eodory foliation of $\Heis(\mathbb{R})$.
        The homogeneous dimension $\Dhom = 4$ (Bass--Guivarch) gives the limiting geometry the
        spectral dimension of a four-dimensional space.
        The corresponding effective metric has Lorentzian signature $(-,+,+,+)$, with one temporal
        direction~$\tau$ and three spatial directions.
        Within the sub-Riemannian structure of $\Heis(\mathbb{R})$, these three spatial directions
        arise as: the two horizontal generators $\tilde X, \tilde Y$ (entering the sub-Riemannian
        Laplacian $\Delta_H = \tilde X^2 + \tilde Y^2$) and the central element
        $\tilde Z = [\tilde X, \tilde Y]$, which enters the effective metric via sub-principal
        corrections (open problem Q5b-O2 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}).

      \paragraph{The admissible ``3'' (O-series).}
        Paper O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} proves that the Born--Infeld fibre admissibility constraint,
        combined with the Hurwitz-to-$\su(2)$ bridge of SpectralCapacity~\cite{Beau2026a2},
        forces the admissible neutral sector to be the imaginary subspace $\ImH \simeq \mathbb{R}^3$
        of the minimal associative non-commutative algebra $\mathbb{H}$.
        This gives $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ as a structural consequence, not an arithmetic input.
        Paper O28~\cite{Beau2026a32} finds $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ (consistent with $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$)
        and rank $r_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3$ for the per-pair covariance.
        Paper O29~\cite{Beau2026a33} identifies the trajectory subspace $\Vr \subset \Heff$ with
        $d_\rho = 2$ via the symmetric rank formula $r_{\mathrm{eff}} = d_\rho(d_\rho+1)/2$,
        confirming the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector.
        Paper O27~\cite{Beau2026a31} proves unconditionally that any admissible morphism factors
        through $\su(2)$, closing the implication chain
        $\text{emergence} \Rightarrow \text{non-injectivity} \Rightarrow \text{pair structure}
        \Rightarrow \text{quadratic form} \Rightarrow \su(2)$.

  \subsection{The Problem}

    Both sub-programmes produce a three-dimensional effective structure.
    Their common substrate is the same group $\Heis(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, but they access it
    through different aspects: the Q-series through the BFS ball geometry, the O-series through
    the representation theory of the Weil blocks.
    The question studied in this paper is:

    \begin{quote}
      \emph{Are the three admissible directions of $\ImH$ (O23) and the three spatial directions
      of the effective Lorentzian geometry (Q5b) the same three-dimensional structure, seen from
      two different vantage points?}
    \end{quote}

    \noindent
    We show below that this question has a precise formulation, admits falsifiability criteria,
    but is \emph{not yet settled} within the corpus.
    The present work does not prove the identification, but shows that if it holds, it is uniquely
    determined by irreducibility (Rigidity Lemma) and must satisfy a specific quadratic-symbol
    constraint (Criterion~\ref{crit:symbol}) whose verification would simultaneously resolve open
    problem Q5b-O2 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}.

  \subsection{Conventions}

    Throughout, $q$ is an odd prime and $G_q = \Heis(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
    We write $\pi_c : V_q \to \Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ for the admissible projection of the
    character-$c$ Weil block, following the notation of O25--O29.
    The Heisenberg Lie algebra is $\heis = \mathrm{span}_\mathbb{R}\{X, Y, Z\}$ with $[X,Y] = Z$
    and $[X,Z] = [Y,Z] = 0$.
    The Lie algebra $\su(2)$ is spanned by $\{e_1, e_2, e_3\}$ with
    $[e_i, e_j] = \varepsilon_{ijk} e_k$ and is identified with $\ImH$ via
    $e_1 \leftrightarrow \mathbf{i}$, $e_2 \leftrightarrow \mathbf{j}$, $e_3 \leftrightarrow \mathbf{k}$.

  \section{Two Independent Notions of Dimension Three}

  \subsection{The Geometric Three: Carnot Grading and Spatial Directions}
    \label{sec:geom3}

    The Heisenberg group $\Heis(\mathbb{R})$ is a Carnot group of step~$2$.
    Its Lie algebra $\heis$ carries a graded decomposition $\heis = V_1 \oplus V_2$ where
    $V_1 = \mathrm{span}\{X, Y\}$ is the horizontal distribution of Carnot degree~$1$ and
    $V_2 = \mathrm{span}\{Z\}$ is the central direction of Carnot degree~$2$.
    The sub-Riemannian Laplacian is $\Delta_H = X^2 + Y^2$ (acting on $\Heis(\mathbb{R})$
    by left-invariant vector fields); it involves \emph{only} the degree-$1$ generators.
    The homogeneous dimension is $\Dhom = 2 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 2 = 4$, giving BFS ball growth
    $|B_n| \sim C n^4$ (Theorem~2.1 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}).

    The effective Lorentzian metric of Theorem~6.1 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b} is
    $g^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, A_z)$ in the basis
    $(\tau, \tilde X, \tilde Y, \tilde Z)$, where:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item $A_\tau > 0$ governs the temporal sector,
      \item $A_H > 0$ governs the two horizontal directions (entering $\Delta_H$ at principal-symbol level),
      \item $A_z > 0$ governs the central direction $\tilde Z$, which enters the effective metric via
      sub-principal corrections to the symbol of $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (open problem Q5b-O2
      of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}).
    \end{itemize}

    \begin{remark}[Horizontal vs.\ derived: two sub-strata of the spatial sector]
      \label{rem:substrata}
      The three spatial directions of $g^{\mu\nu}$ do \emph{not} form a homogeneous set from the
      sub-Riemannian viewpoint.
      The directions $\tilde X, \tilde Y$ are horizontal (Carnot degree~$1$) and enter the effective
      operator at the level of the principal symbol.
      The direction $\tilde Z = [\tilde X, \tilde Y]$ is derived (Carnot degree~$2$) and is invisible
      to the principal symbol of $\Delta_H$; it contributes to the effective metric only through
      sub-principal corrections.
      This two-level structure --- degree-$1$ directions contributing at leading order, degree-$2$
      direction contributing at sub-leading order --- will play a key role in the bridge construction
      of Section~\ref{sec:bridge}, where it corresponds precisely to the distinction between
      spin-weight $\pm 1$ and spin-weight $0$ in the triplet $\Sym^2(\Vr)$.
    \end{remark}

    The three-dimensional spatial sector of $g^{\mu\nu}$ is the direct sum
    $V_1 \oplus V_2 = \heis / \mathbb{R}\tau$, where $\tau$ denotes the projective temporal direction.
    The ``geometric~$3$'' is $\dim_\mathbb{R}(V_1 \oplus V_2) = 3$.

  \subsection{The Admissible Three: Quaternionic Minimality}
    \label{sec:adm3}

    The admissible ``3'' arises at the representation-theoretic level.
    Paper O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} proves that the unique minimal associative non-commutative
    algebra compatible with Born--Infeld fibre admissibility is $\mathbb{H}$, and that the
    admissible neutral directions correspond to $\ImH$, which carries exactly three independent
    axes.
    The saturation threshold $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ is a consequence of this quaternionic minimality,
    not an arithmetic input.

    At the fibre level, the admissible projection $\pi_c : V_q \to \Heff$ lands in
    $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$, consistently with $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$~\cite{Beau2026a32}.
    The per-pair covariance $C_c$ in $\mathrm{End}(\Heff)$ has rank $r_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3$, filling
    $\Heff$ completely~\cite{Beau2026a32}.
    Paper O29~\cite{Beau2026a33} identifies the trajectory subspace $\Vr \subset \Heff$ with
    $d_\rho = 2$ via the symmetric rank formula; $\Heff$ then appears as the space of degree-two
    symmetric tensors over $\Vr$, i.e.\ $\Heff \simeq \Sym^2_\mathbb{C}(\Vr)$ as a vector space.
    The ``admissible~3'' is the complex dimension of $\Heff$ (equivalently, the rank of $C_c$).

    \begin{remark}[Three distinct ``3''s to be kept separate]
      \label{rem:three3s}
      The present paper distinguishes three a priori distinct occurrences of~$3$ in the programme:
      (i) $\dim_\mathbb{R}(\heis / \mathbb{R}\tau) = 3$ (geometric, from Carnot structure),
      (ii) $\dim_\mathbb{R}(\ImH) = 3$ (algebraic, from quaternionic minimality, O23),
      (iii) $\dim_\mathbb{C}(\Heff) = r_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3$ (representation-theoretic, from O28).
      These coincide numerically but need not coincide structurally.
      Paper O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} Section~1.2 already flags a similar cautionary remark
      within the O-series itself.
    \end{remark}

  \section{The Algebraic Obstruction: $\su(2) \not\simeq \heis$}
  \label{sec:obstruction}

  \begin{proposition}[Non-isomorphism]
    \label{prop:noniso}
    The Lie algebras $\su(2) \simeq \ImH$ and $\heis = \mathfrak{heis}_3$ are non-isomorphic.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    The algebra $\su(2)$ is semisimple (its Killing form is negative-definite); $\heis$ is
    nilpotent of class~$2$ (its derived ideal $[\heis, \heis] = \mathrm{span}\{Z\}$ is central and
    non-trivial).
    A semisimple Lie algebra cannot be nilpotent.
  \end{proof}

  \noindent
  Proposition~\ref{prop:noniso} shows that the admissible ``3'' (which lives in $\su(2)$-space)
  and the geometric ``3'' (which lives in $\heis$-space) cannot be identified by a Lie algebra
  isomorphism.
  Any bridge between them must therefore operate at a level where the two structures are
  \emph{compatible without being isomorphic}: this is precisely the level of representations.

  \section{Structural Constraints on Any Bridge}
  \label{sec:bridge}

  \subsection{The Symmetric Square Identification}
    \label{sec:sym2}

    Paper O29~\cite{Beau2026a33} establishes $d_\rho = 2$, so $\Vr \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ as
    an $\su(2)$-module: this is the unique irreducible spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ representation.
    The symmetric square $\Sym^2_\mathbb{C}(\Vr)$ is the irreducible spin-$1$ representation of
    $\su(2)$, of complex dimension~$3$.
    It has a canonical basis $\{e_+, e_0, e_-\}$ of weight eigenstates under the Cartan generator
    $J_3$: $J_3 e_{\pm} = \pm e_{\pm}$, $J_3 e_0 = 0$.

    \begin{proposition}[Symmetric square identification]
      \label{prop:sym2}
      As an $\su(2)$-module, $\Heff \simeq \Sym^2(\Vr)$ is the unique irreducible three-dimensional
      representation of $\su(2)$, with highest weight~$1$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Paper O28~\cite{Beau2026a32} shows that the per-pair outer products $M_j = w_j w_j^T$ span
      $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$, where $w_j = \pi_c(v_j) \in \Vr \subset \Heff$.
      The map $\Vr \otimes \Vr \to \Heff$ given by $u \otimes v \mapsto uv^T$ has image contained in the
      complex symmetric matrices $\Sym^2_\mathbb{C}(\Vr)$, since $M_j = w_j w_j^T$ is symmetric.
      Paper O29~\cite{Beau2026a33} establishes $r_{\mathrm{eff}} = d_\rho(d_\rho+1)/2 = 3 = \dim \Sym^2(\Vr)$,
      so the image spans $\Sym^2_\mathbb{C}(\Vr)$.
      Since $\Vr \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ is the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ representation, the symmetric square
      is the spin-$1$ representation by the standard Clebsch--Gordan decomposition
      $\mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^2 = \Sym^2 \oplus \wedge^2 = V_1 \oplus V_0$.
    \end{proof}

    \noindent
    Proposition~\ref{prop:sym2} is not explicit in O28--O29, where $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ appears
    only as the ambient admissible projection space; the $\su(2)$-module structure is inferred here
    from the symmetric rank formula.

  \subsection{Grading Correspondence}
    \label{sec:grading}

    The spin-weight grading on $\Sym^2(\Vr) = \mathrm{span}\{e_+, e_0, e_-\}$ decomposes the three
    basis states according to their $J_3$-eigenvalue $m \in \{+1, 0, -1\}$.
    The Carnot grading on $\heis$ decomposes the generators according to their homogeneous degree
    $d \in \{1, 2\}$.
    These are distinct gradings on distinct spaces, but they are compatible in the following sense.

    \begin{remark}[Grading correspondence]
      \label{rem:grading}
      The pair $(m, d) = (\pm 1, 1)$ (spin $\pm 1$, Carnot degree $1$) corresponds to the horizontal
      generators $\tilde X, \tilde Y$, and the pair $(m, d) = (0, 2)$ (spin $0$, Carnot degree $2$)
      corresponds to the central generator $\tilde Z = [\tilde X, \tilde Y]$.
      The pattern is: the two generators entering the principal symbol of $\Delta_H$ (at leading order)
      have $|m| = 1$, while the generator entering via sub-principal corrections (at sub-leading order)
      has $m = 0$.
      This grading correspondence is not a Lie algebra isomorphism (Proposition~\ref{prop:noniso}), but
      it shows that the two sub-strata of the spatial sector identified in Remark~\ref{rem:substrata}
      map to the two spin-weight sectors of the spin-$1$ triplet in a grading-compatible way.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Rigidity of Any Bridge: Schur's Lemma}
    \label{sec:schur}

    The irreducibility of $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ as an $\su(2)$-module has an immediate and strong consequence
    for the possible forms of any bridge to the spatial sector.

    \begin{lemma}[Rigidity via Schur]
      \label{lem:schur}
      Let $W$ be a three-dimensional real vector space equipped with an $\su(2)$-action that decomposes
      as $W \simeq \mathfrak{su}(2)$ (adjoint representation, i.e.\ spin-$1$ over $\mathbb{R}$).
      Any $\su(2)$-equivariant $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $\varphi : \Sym^2(\Vr) \to W \otimes_\mathbb{R}
      \mathbb{C}$ is either zero or a scalar multiple of the unique (up to phase) isomorphism.
      In particular, the image of $\varphi$ is all of $W \otimes \mathbb{C}$, and the map is
      determined up to a single complex scalar.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      The module $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ is irreducible of dimension~$3$ over $\mathbb{C}$.
      The complexification $W \otimes_\mathbb{R} \mathbb{C}$ of the real spin-$1$ representation is
      also irreducible of dimension~$3$ over $\mathbb{C}$ (the complexification of the adjoint
      of $\su(2)$ is the spin-$1$ representation of $\sl_2(\mathbb{C})$).
      By Schur's lemma, $\mathrm{Hom}_{\su(2)}(\Sym^2(\Vr), W \otimes \mathbb{C}) \simeq \mathbb{C}$:
      the space of equivariant maps is one-dimensional, so any non-zero equivariant map is an isomorphism,
      unique up to a non-zero scalar.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Consequence for the bridge]
      \label{rem:rigidity}
      Lemma~\ref{lem:schur} implies that \emph{there is at most one equivariant bridge} (up to overall
      normalisation) between $\Heff = \Sym^2(\Vr)$ and the spatial sector of the effective metric.
      This is a strong rigidity result: the bridge, if it exists, is not a matter of choice.
      Its existence is the open question; its uniqueness is guaranteed by irreducibility.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{The Schr\"odinger Quadratic Form Lemma}
    \label{sec:qform}

    We now compute the quadratic form induced by the $\su(2)$-Casimir on $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ and show
    that its pullback under any equivariant bridge is positive-definite on the spatial sector.

    The Casimir operator of $\su(2)$ on the spin-$j$ irreducible representation is
    $\mathcal{C} = j(j+1) \cdot \mathrm{Id}$.
    On $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ (spin $j = 1$), this gives $\mathcal{C}|_{\Sym^2} = 2 \cdot \mathrm{Id}$.

    \begin{lemma}[Schr\"{o}dinger quadratic form]
      \label{lem:qform}
      Let $\varphi : \Sym^2(\Vr) \xrightarrow{\sim} W_{\mathrm{sp}}$ be any $\su(2)$-equivariant
      isomorphism to the spatial sector $W_{\mathrm{sp}}$ of the effective metric (the unique such map
      up to scalar, by Lemma~\ref{lem:schur}).
      Then:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item The quadratic form $Q_{\mathcal{C}}(v) := \langle v, \mathcal{C} v \rangle = 2\|v\|^2$
        on $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ is positive-definite of rank~$3$.
        \item Under any equivariant bridge $\varphi$, the pullback $\varphi^* g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ (where
        $g_{\mathrm{sp}} = \mathrm{diag}(A_H, A_H, A_z)$ is the spatial block of $g^{\mu\nu}$) is also
        positive-definite of rank~$3$.
        \item The induced map on quadratic forms is:
        \begin{equation}
          \varphi^* g_{\mathrm{sp}} = \lambda_H \cdot Q_{\pm} + \lambda_z \cdot Q_0,
        \end{equation}
        where $Q_{\pm}$ is the projection onto the spin-weight $\pm 1$ eigenspace $\{e_+, e_-\}$,
        $Q_0$ is the projection onto the spin-weight $0$ eigenspace $\{e_0\}$,
        and $\lambda_H = A_H / \|\varphi\|^2$, $\lambda_z = A_z / \|\varphi\|^2$ are positive constants.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      Item~(1) is immediate from $\mathcal{C} = 2 \cdot \mathrm{Id}$ and $\dim \Sym^2(\Vr) = 3$.
      For items~(2) and~(3): the spatial metric $g_{\mathrm{sp}} = \mathrm{diag}(A_H, A_H, A_z)$
      in the basis $(\tilde X, \tilde Y, \tilde Z)$ preserves the sub-strata of Remark~\ref{rem:substrata}:
      it acts by $A_H$ on $V_1 = \mathrm{span}\{\tilde X, \tilde Y\}$ (Carnot degree~$1$) and by $A_z$
      on $V_2 = \mathrm{span}\{\tilde Z\}$ (Carnot degree~$2$).
      The absence of cross terms between $V_1$ and $V_2$ in $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ follows from the
      $\mathrm{U}(1)$ symmetry generated by $J_3$: the rotation $\tilde X \leftrightarrow \tilde Y$
      (sending $e_+ \leftrightarrow e_-$) preserves $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ (since $A_H = A_H$) and fixes
      $e_0$ (and hence $\tilde Z$), so no $J_3$-equivariant quadratic form can mix the weight-$\pm 1$
      sector with the weight-$0$ sector.
      This $\mathrm{U}(1)$ argument holds independently of the existence of the full bridge $\varphi$;
      it is a consequence of the isotropic admissible regime assumed in Theorem~6.1 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}.
      Under the grading correspondence of Remark~\ref{rem:grading}, $V_1$ maps to the spin-weight
      $\pm 1$ sector $\{e_+, e_-\}$ and $V_2$ maps to the spin-weight $0$ sector $\{e_0\}$.
      The pullback $\varphi^* g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ therefore acts by $A_H / \|\varphi\|^2$ on $\{e_+, e_-\}$
      and by $A_z / \|\varphi\|^2$ on $\{e_0\}$, giving~(3).
      Since $A_H, A_z > 0$ (established in Theorem~6.1 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}), the pullback is
      positive-definite, giving~(2).
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Status of Lemma~\ref{lem:qform}]
Items~(1) and the positivity assertion of~(2) are structural and hold unconditionally.
Item~(3) and the full form of~(2) are conditional on the existence of the equivariant bridge
$\varphi$ (i.e.\ on the truth of Conjecture~\ref{conj:bridge} below) and on Theorem~6.1
of~\cite{Beau2026q5b} (which is itself conditional on~[H-lift]).
The lemma is therefore to be read as: \emph{if the bridge exists, it is isometric in the
graded sense} --- and in that case the spatial metric is uniquely determined up to the two
constants $A_H$ and $A_z$.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{The Bridge Conjecture}
    \label{sec:conj}

    The results above constrain the form of any equivariant bridge.
    We now state the remaining open question as a falsifiable conjecture.

    \begin{conjecture}[Bridge existence]
      \label{conj:bridge}
      There exists an $\su(2)$-equivariant isomorphism
      \begin{equation}
        \varphi : \Heff = \Sym^2(\Vr) \xrightarrow{\sim} W_{\mathrm{sp}},
      \end{equation}
      where $W_{\mathrm{sp}} = \mathrm{span}\{\tilde X, \tilde Y, \tilde Z\} = \heis/\mathbb{R}\tau$
      is the spatial sector of the effective metric, compatible with the Schr\"{o}dinger representation
      in the following sense.
      The compatibility is understood at the level of quadratic forms induced by the generators,
      not at the level of Lie algebra homomorphisms (which do not exist, by
      Proposition~\ref{prop:noniso}).
      Concretely: the quadratic form $Q_{\mathcal{C}} = 2\|\cdot\|^2$ on $\Sym^2(\Vr)$, when transported
      to $W_{\mathrm{sp}}$ via $\varphi$, matches the spatial block of $\sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})$
      restricted to the admissible sector of $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ in the large-$q$ limit
      of Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.
    \end{conjecture}

    \noindent
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:schur}, if this map exists it is unique up to scalar.
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:qform}, if it exists the spatial metric $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is positive-definite
    and takes the graded form~(3), with $A_H$ controlling the horizontal sector and $A_z$ controlling
    the central sector.
    The conjecture is therefore not an additional degree of freedom: it is a binary existence question
    whose answer either closes the identification of the two~``3''s or definitively separates them.

  \section{Falsifiability Criterion}
  \label{sec:falsif}

  The Rigidity Lemma~\ref{lem:schur} reduces the question to a single binary test.
  Since the bridge, if it exists, is unique up to scalar, there is no ambiguity about what to test.

  \begin{criterion}[Symbol restriction to $\Sym^2(\Vr)$]
    \label{crit:symbol}
    Let $\sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})$ be the principal symbol of the effective operator
    $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ on $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \Heis(\mathbb{R})$, evaluated on the spatial block
    $(k_X, k_Y, k_Z)$.
    Via the grading correspondence of Remark~\ref{rem:grading}, identify the spatial cotangent
    directions $(k_X, k_Y, k_Z)$ with the dual basis of $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ via the spin-weight
    assignment $k_X \leftrightarrow e_+$, $k_Y \leftrightarrow e_-$, $k_Z \leftrightarrow e_0$.
    Test whether:
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})\big|_{\Sym^2(\Vr)} = A_H (k_X^2 + k_Y^2) + A_z k_Z^2,
      \quad A_H, A_z > 0.
    \end{equation}
    If this identity holds (with $A_z$ arising from sub-principal corrections as in Q5b-O2), then
    Conjecture~\ref{conj:bridge} is confirmed, the two ``3''s are identified, and Q5b-O2 is resolved.
    If the identity fails --- either because the restriction has rank less than~$3$, or because the
    symbol mixes the spin-weight sectors --- the bridge does not exist and the two ``3''s are
    structurally distinct.
  \end{criterion}

  \begin{remark}[Computability and partial analytical verification]
    \label{rem:compute}
    Criterion~\ref{crit:symbol} has now been partially resolved.
    Section~\ref{sec:numerical} proves analytically that $[F_c, L_{\mathrm{Weil}}] = 0$
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:commute}), which implies that the cross terms $k_X k_Z$ and $k_Y k_Z$
    vanish structurally for all $(q,c)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:blockdiag}).
    Numerical verification on O25 checkpoints for $q \in \{61,101,151\}$ confirms this for
    $c \in \{3,5,7\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:numerical}).
    The remaining open part of Criterion~\ref{crit:symbol} is the isotropy condition $A_H = A_z$:
    whether the two horizontal directions and the central direction carry equal effective-metric
    coefficients requires the sub-principal analysis of Q5b-O2 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}.
  \end{remark}

  \subsection{Metaplectic symmetry of the discrete Weil Laplacian}
    \label{sec:metatplectic}

    The Weil Laplacian $L_{\mathrm{Weil}}$ on $\mathbb{C}^q$ for character $c$ is
    \begin{equation}
      L_{\mathrm{Weil}} = 4I - W_a - W_a^\dagger - W_b - W_b^\dagger,
    \end{equation}
    where $W_a$ is the cyclic shift $(W_a f)(k) = f(k-1 \bmod q)$ and
    $W_b = \mathrm{diag}(e^{2\pi i c k/q})_{k=0}^{q-1}$ is the character-$c$ phase operator.
    The chirped discrete Fourier transform $F_c$ is defined by
    \begin{equation}
      (F_c)_{jk} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\, e^{2\pi i c jk/q}, \qquad j,k \in \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}.
    \end{equation}
    It is the metaplectic representative of the symplectic rotation $(x,\xi) \mapsto (-\xi, x)$
    in the character-$c$ Weil representation:
    $F_c W_a F_c^\dagger = W_b^\dagger$ and $F_c W_b F_c^\dagger = W_a^\dagger$.

    \begin{proposition}[Metaplectic symmetry]
      \label{prop:commute}
      For every odd prime $q$ and every $c$ with $\gcd(c,q) = 1$, the chirped DFT $F_c$ commutes with
      the Weil Laplacian:
      \[
        [F_c,\, L_{\mathrm{Weil}}] = 0.
      \]
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      From the defining properties of $F_c$:
      \[
        F_c\, L_{\mathrm{Weil}}\, F_c^\dagger
        = 4I - F_c W_a F_c^\dagger - F_c W_a^\dagger F_c^\dagger
        - F_c W_b F_c^\dagger - F_c W_b^\dagger F_c^\dagger
        = 4I - W_b^\dagger - W_b - W_a^\dagger - W_a = L_{\mathrm{Weil}}.
      \]
      Hence $F_c L_{\mathrm{Weil}} = L_{\mathrm{Weil}} F_c$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Block-diagonal structure]
      \label{cor:blockdiag}
      The matrix $L_{\mathrm{Weil}}$ is block-diagonal in every $F_c$-eigenbasis.
      Consequently, for any admissible basis $B_{\mathrm{eff}} \in \mathbb{C}^{3 \times q}$ whose rows
      lie in distinct $F_c$-eigenspaces, the restriction
      $\tilde{L} = B_{\mathrm{eff}}\, L_{\mathrm{Weil}}\, B_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger$ has no off-diagonal
      entries between sectors of different $F_c$-weight.
      In particular, the cross terms $k_X k_Z$ and $k_Y k_Z$ in $\sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})$ are
      \emph{structurally zero}, independently of $q$ and $c$.
    \end{corollary}

    \noindent
    Corollary~\ref{cor:blockdiag} proves the ``no cross terms'' part of
    Criterion~\ref{crit:symbol} analytically.
    This holds for all $(q,c)$ with $\gcd(c,q)=1$; verification by computation is therefore
    in principle redundant for the cross terms, and the numerical campaign below confirms it.

      For $c \in \{3,7\}$ (low-energy sector), the isotropy gap converges:

      \label{cor:quasithm}
      The following hold unconditionally or with strong numerical support:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item \emph{(Proved)} The effective symbol restricted to $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ has no cross terms:
        \[
          \sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})\big|_{H_{\mathrm{eff}}} = A_H(k_X^2 + k_Y^2) + A_z k_Z^2.
        \]
        \item \emph{(Numerical, $q \in \{61,101,151\}$)} The Z-sector coefficient is universal:
        $A_z = 2 = C_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$.
        \item \emph{(Numerical, $q \in \{61,101,151\}$)} The horizontal gap satisfies
        $|A_H - A_z| < 0.05$ for $c \in \{3,7\}$, decreasing with $q$.
      \end{enumerate}
      If Conjecture~\ref{conj:isotropy} holds, then $A_H = A_z = C_{\mathfrak{su}(2)} = 2$ in the
      large-$q$ limit, Conjecture~\ref{conj:bridge} is confirmed, and the three spatial dimensions
      are identified with the spin-$1$ representation of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ with the effective
      spatial metric governed entirely by the Casimir:
      \[
        g_{\mathrm{sp}} = C_{\mathfrak{su}(2)} \cdot (dk_X^2 + dk_Y^2 + dk_Z^2).
      \]
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{remark}[Three-case structure of the isotropy question]
      \label{rem:threecases}
      Criterion~\ref{crit:symbol} in its original form requires both the vanishing of cross terms and
      the equality $A_H = A_z$.
      The first condition is now settled (Corollary~\ref{cor:blockdiag}).
      For the second, three genuinely distinct outcomes are possible, with separate physical implications.
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item $A_H = A_z > 0$: the bridge exists and the effective spatial geometry is isotropic.
        The three spatial directions are metrically equivalent.
        This is the strongest conclusion: the two ``3''s are fully identified.
        \item $A_H \neq A_z$, both positive: the bridge exists but the effective spatial geometry is
        anisotropic.
        The three spatial directions are metrically distinct ($A_H$ for the horizontal, $A_z$ for the
        central).
        This is still a positive result: the diagonal structure is confirmed and the bridge is real,
        but the spatial metric is genuinely non-isotropic.
        \item $A_z \leq 0$ or structurally unstable: the bridge does not exist.
        The two ``3''s are structurally independent.
      \end{enumerate}
      The numerical data (Table~\ref{tab:numerical}) support case~(1) or case~(2) for $c \in \{3,7\}$:
      $A_z \approx 2$ and $A_H \approx 2$ with $|A_H - A_z|/A_z < 0.05$ for all tested $(q,c)$ in
      the low-energy sector, pointing strongly to case~(1).
      Case~(3) is not observed.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Discussion}
  \label{sec:disc}

  \subsection{What This Paper Establishes}

    The following results are proved unconditionally or structurally in this paper:

    \begin{itemize}
      \item The two occurrences of~$3$ in the programme arise via distinct mechanisms at distinct
      algebraic levels and cannot be identified by a Lie algebra isomorphism
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:noniso}).
      \item The admissible projection space $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ carries the structure of the unique
      irreducible three-dimensional $\su(2)$-module $\Sym^2(\Vr)$, inferred from O29
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:sym2}).
      \item Any equivariant bridge between $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ and the spatial sector is unique up to
      positive scalar (Lemma~\ref{lem:schur}): the bridge is rigid, not a matter of convention.
      \item The quadratic form induced by the $\su(2)$-Casimir on $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ pulls back, under any
      equivariant bridge, to a positive-definite graded form on the spatial sector that matches
      $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ up to the two constants $A_H$ and $A_z$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:qform}).
      \item The grading correspondence (Remark~\ref{rem:grading}) connects the Carnot degree-$(1,2)$
      decomposition of $\heis$ to the spin-weight-$(\pm 1, 0)$ decomposition of $\Sym^2(\Vr)$,
      explaining why the $\tilde Z$ direction enters at sub-principal order in the effective metric.
      \item The no-cross-terms part of this criterion is proved analytically
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:commute} and Corollary~\ref{cor:blockdiag}) and confirmed numerically
      on O25 checkpoints (Table~\ref{tab:numerical}).
      The remaining open part is the isotropy condition $A_H = A_z$, with numerical evidence
      pointing to case~(1) of Remark~\ref{rem:threecases}: $A_H \approx A_z \approx 2$ in the
      low-energy sector, decreasing gap (Table~\ref{tab:asymp}), and a power-law fit
      consistent with convergence $A_H \to A_z$ as $q \to \infty$
      (Conjecture~\ref{conj:isotropy}).
      \item The universal value $A_z = 2 = C_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$ is a spectral signature of the bridge:
      it matches the eigenvalue of the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ Casimir on $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$
      (Remark~\ref{rem:az2}).
      If Conjecture~\ref{conj:isotropy} holds, the bridge identification reduces to the statement
      that the effective spatial metric is governed by the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ Casimir
      (Corollary~\ref{cor:quasithm}).
    \end{itemize}

  \subsection{Relation to Open Problem Q5b-O2}

    Open problem Q5b-O2 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b} asks for the extension of the effective metric
    to a full $4\times 4$ Lorentzian tensor including the $\tilde Z$-direction, via sub-principal
    corrections to the symbol of $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
    The present paper provides the representation-theoretic prediction: if the bridge exists, the
    $\tilde Z$-direction contributes a term $A_z k_Z^2$ to the spatial block of the symbol, without
    cross terms with $(k_X, k_Y)$.
    The absence of cross terms is no longer a prediction but a theorem
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:commute}).
    The target for Q5b-O2 is therefore sharpened: compute the sub-principal symbol in the $\tilde Z$
    direction and determine whether $A_z$ equals the Casimir value $C_{\mathfrak{su}(2)} = 2$
    (Remark~\ref{rem:az2}).
    Numerical evidence for $q \in \{61, 101, 151\}$ consistently gives $A_z \approx 2$,
    providing a computable target for the analytic computation.

  \subsection{If the Identification Succeeds}

    If Criterion~\ref{crit:symbol} holds, the programme would have derived a structural identification
    candidate between the three spatial directions of the effective geometry and the three-dimensional
    irreducible representation of the minimal admissible non-commutative algebra $\mathbb{H}$.
    The three-dimensionality of space would follow from the uniqueness of the spin-$1$ representation
    of $\su(2)$ (itself forced by Born--Infeld admissibility, O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}).
    The tag \texttt{spt} would then be retroactively justified on O23, O28, and O29.

  \subsection{If the Identification Fails}

    A failure of Criterion~\ref{crit:symbol} --- either through rank deficiency or through cross terms
    mixing the spin-weight sectors --- would imply that $\Heff$ is an internal representation space
    not identifiable with the spatial sector of the effective geometry.
    This would be a significant negative result: it would bound the scope of the O-series, establish
    that the two ``3''s are structurally independent coincidences, and sharpen the open problem for
    a future paper.

  \section{Conclusion}

  The programme exhibits two independent structural outputs equal to the integer~$3$: the Carnot
  spatial sector of $\Heis(\mathbb{R})$ via the Bass--Guivarch homogeneous dimension, and the
  admissible effective space $\Heff = \mathbb{C}^3$ via quaternionic minimality and the symmetric
  rank formula.
  These two ``3''s are numerically equal but algebraically distinct: $\su(2) \not\simeq \heis$.
  The present paper establishes three structural results that constrain any possible bridge between
  them.
  First, $\Heff \simeq \Sym^2(\Vr)$ as an $\su(2)$-module, making the admissible ``3'' the
  dimension of the spin-$1$ representation.
  Second, the irreducibility of $\Sym^2(\Vr)$ forces any equivariant bridge to be unique up to
  scalar (Rigidity Lemma), removing all conventional freedom from the identification.
  Third, the $\su(2)$-Casimir pulls back to a positive-definite graded quadratic form on the
  spatial sector, matching the structure of the effective metric $g_{\mathrm{sp}}$ with the spin-weight
  grading reproducing the Carnot grading of $\heis$ (Schr\"{o}dinger Quadratic Form Lemma).
  The open question is thereby reduced to a single computable criterion
  (Criterion~\ref{crit:symbol}) on the principal symbol of $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$, which simultaneously
  constitutes a resolution strategy for open problem Q5b-O2 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}.
  The no-cross-terms part of this criterion is now settled: we prove analytically that
  $[F_c, L_{\mathrm{Weil}}] = 0$ for all $(q,c)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:commute}), which forces
  the cross terms between spin-weight sectors to vanish structurally; numerical verification on
  O25 checkpoints for $q \in \{61, 101, 151\}$ is consistent with this result (Table~\ref{tab:numerical}).
  The remaining open question is whether $A_H = A_z$ (isotropy condition,
  Conjecture~\ref{conj:isotropy}).
  Numerical evidence gives $A_z \approx A_H \approx 2$ in the low-energy sector ($c \in \{3,7\}$),
  with the universal value $A_z = 2$ matching the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ Casimir eigenvalue on
  $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:az2}).
  A power-law fit of $|A_H - A_z|$ over $q \in \{61,101,151\}$ for $c=7$ gives an exponent
  $\approx -0.44$, consistent with convergence to zero as $q \to \infty$ (Table~\ref{tab:asymp}).
  The identification of the two ``3''s is thereby reduced to an asymptotic scalar equality for
  which all current analytical and numerical evidence is positive; verification at
  $q \in \{211, 307, 401\}$ would establish that the effective spatial metric is governed
  entirely by the Casimir $C_{\mathfrak{su}(2)} = 2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:quasithm}).
